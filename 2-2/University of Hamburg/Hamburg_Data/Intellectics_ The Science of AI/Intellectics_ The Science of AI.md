### [Beschreibung des Studiengangs](javascript:void(0) "Inhalt verbergen")

The aim of the research-oriented and entirely English-language programme "Intellectics M.A." is to provide students with both a in-depth understanding of the foundations, methods, and results of the science of artificial intelligence (AI) as well as a broad scientific understanding of the interaction of humans with systems that can perform non-trivial tasks for humans. Approaches will be pursued that arise from relevant questions in the humanities, including philosophy, contributing to solving the above-described problems.  

Artificial Intelligence (AI) is an interdisciplinary field of research that uses the findings of various humanities and structural sciences (in addition to engineering and natural sciences) and implements and develops them from an information technology perspective. The paradigm of a rational agent (also called an agent system) is central to the teaching of information technology abstraction and modelling. In this paradigm, an agent acts in a dynamic environment that independently develops a plan to achieve an internal goal with limited time and memory resources, depending on its observations and its internally formed models. An agent may develop internal goals from externally provided task descriptions or might also derive goals proactively within the interaction context of an overall multi-agent system. Intelligence in the sense of Intellectics means that agents act rationally and fulfil non-trivial tasks to the satisfaction of humans without using a predefined algorithm and without permanent supervision. Agents might mimic humans or might go beyond human capabilities in certain respects. In systems with multiple agents and humans, additional challenges arise because different local goals may be incompatible with a global goal. A system that successfully achieves a globally defined goal is called an intelligent AI system.  

In addition to a critical and analytical examination of intelligent agents, students will acquire the theoretical and practical skills necessary to design new agents that are safe, trustworthy, robust, transparent and accountable. Moreover, students will learn to evaluate a wide range of design possibilities for human-agent interaction contexts and respective potential risks by reflecting on social mechanisms and ethical issues.

### [Berufliche Perspektiven](javascript:void(0) "Inhalt anzeigen")

In terms of career prospects, students not only have the option of pursuing an academic career with a PhD at the interface of AI, e.g. with computer science, philosophy, mathematics, sociology, economics, etc. But they also obtain the option of working in a consultancy position for companies, commissions or non-government organisations on all AI-related topics. The declared goal of the programme in terms of sustainable teaching is not only to help students to successfully apply for jobs in the above-mentioned fields, but also to ensure quality and stability in professional life (job security). This is achieved by sustainably imparting the essential ideas, concepts, methods, and approaches in contrast to a single focus on currently trendy technologies, programming languages or the like.

### [Studienaufbau](javascript:void(0) "Inhalt anzeigen")

The degree program in Intellectics M.A. comprises a total of 120 credit points (CPs)m, which are distributed across the two parts of the program as follows:

1. Subject-specific modules (including the final module) in the compulsory area (102 CPs)
2. Elective area (18 CPs)

The compulsory part of the Master's programme consists of nine compulsory modules, each worth 8 credit points, and the final module, including the Master's thesis, worth 30 credit points. During the first three semesters, students acquire 24 credits in the compulsory part. This leaves 6 CPs per semester in the first three semesters for the elective part, which students acquire through successful participation in selected Master courses at the University of Hamburg (participation in the relevant modules must be confirmed). In the second summer semester of the curriculum, the final module including the Master's thesis (30 CPs) is foreseen.  

The compulsory modules are divided into three pillars. The content of the pillars can be summarised as follows:  

Pillar 1: This pillar aims at developing data and algorithmic competence. In addition to the basics of probability theory and the approaches of classical algorithms of machine learning as well as their practical use and implementation, the basic ideas of modern (large) language and image processing models and their multimodal combination are taught. Different approaches are used to explain how complex functions can be broken down into smaller ones and how these can be systematically parameterised with the help of training data to achieve the desired functionality. Approaches to verifiably correct behaviour of such systems for the benefit of humans are discussed. Knowledge from applied mathematics and linguistics will also be incorporated.  

Pillar 2: This pillar focuses on the intelligent agent as a system that plans actions and makes decisions. It is conveyed that communication can also be interpreted as action. In addition to well-known classical planning methods, advanced probabilistic models will be discussed that can be used to generate and execute (optimal) plans for complex goals, as required, for example, for implementation in generative AI systems. Insights from applied mathematics and philosophy (agency, decision theory, temporal logic) will be incorporated.   

Pillar 3: The content of this pillar focuses on the multi-agent aspect of the Intellectics M.A. programme. Relevant approaches from game theory, the design of social mechanisms, and formal ethics are taught from a logic-centred perspective. Insights from applied mathematics, sociology (social mechanisms), philosophy (epistemology, approaches to causality, philosophy of mind), linguistics (conditionals), psychology (behaviour of people in a group in the context of a game) and ethics (normative principles and their implementation, e.g. in deontic logic) are incorporated.

## Introduction

The aim of the research-oriented and English-language program "Intellectics M.A. is to provide students with a deep understanding of the foundations, approaches, methods and results of the science of artificial intelligence (AI), based on the humanistic principle of education, and to promote a broad scientific understanding of the interaction between humans and systems that can perform non-trivial tasks for humans. Approaches for understanding these interactions with mutual influences will be pursued, assuming, for example, a perspective with research questions from the humanities, including philosophy.

The science of AI is an interdisciplinary field of research that uses findings from various humanities and structural sciences (in addition to engineering and natural sciences) and implements them from an information technology perspective. Central to the abstraction and modelling of information technology is the paradigm of a rational agent (also known as an agentic system). According to this paradigm, an actor acts in a dynamic environment and, given limited time and memory resources, independently develops a plan to achieve an internal goal based on its observations and its internally formed models. An agent may develop internal goals from context and task descriptions, or proactively within the framework of an overall context in which humans interact with agents. Intelligence in the sense of intellect means that agents act rationally and fulfil non-trivial tasks to the satisfaction of humans without using a predefined algorithm. In systems with multiple agents and humans, additional challenges arise because different local goals may be incompatible with a global goal. A system that successfully achieves a global goal is called an intelligent or AI system.

In addition to a critical-analytical examination of intelligent agents, students will acquire the theoretical and practical skills necessary to develop new agents that are safe, trustworthy, robust, transparent and explainable. Furthermore, they will learn to evaluate a wide range of possibilities and potential dangers by reflecting on ethical issues with a view on social mechanisms (interaction of human and agents with mutual influences).

Professionally, students not only have the option of pursuing an academic career with a doctorate in the interdisciplinary field of AI, e.g. with computer science, philosophy, mathematics, sociology, economics, etc. But, also, they may opt for working in an advisory capacity, e.g., in companies, commissions or non-profit organisations for all AI-relevant topics. The declared aim of the program in terms of sustainable teaching is not only to help students to successfully apply for jobs
