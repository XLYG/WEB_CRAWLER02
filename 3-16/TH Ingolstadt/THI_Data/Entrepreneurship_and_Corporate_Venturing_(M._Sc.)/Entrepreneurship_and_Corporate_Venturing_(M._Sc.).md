**Willkommen zum Master „Entrepreneurship and Corporate Venturing“ an der THI Business School!**

Unser M. Sc. in Entrepreneurship and Corporate Venturing ist ein **innovativer, konsekutiver Masterstudiengang**, der sich an Absolvierende wirtschaftswissenschaftlicher oder verwandter Bachelorprogramme richtet. Mit einem klaren Fokus auf **Unternehmertum** und **Corporate Venturing** lernen Sie, wie Sie **Start-ups** und **digitale Geschäftsmodelle** strategisch entwickeln, skalieren und erfolgreich umsetzen.

**Warum „Entrepreneurship and Corporate Venturing“ studieren?**

Unser Studiengang vermittelt Ihnen **inter- und multidisziplinäres Wissen**, das Sie auf eine Karriere in der Gründung eigener Start-ups, in der Weiterentwicklung von **Familienunternehmen** oder im **Innovationsmanagement** etablierter Unternehmen vorbereitet.

**Zu den zentralen Inhalten gehören:**

* **Strategie und Wachstum:** Entwicklung und Steuerung innovativer Geschäftsmodelle und digitaler Produkte
* **Corporate Venturing**: Aufbau unternehmerischer Initiativen innerhalb bestehender Unternehmen
* **Qualitative Marktforschung**: Identifizierung neuer Geschäftsmöglichkeiten
* **Quantitative Datenanalyse**: Validierung von Konzepten mit Primär- und Sekundärdaten
* **Innovations- und Technologietransfer:** Anwendung von Forschungsergebnissen in marktfähige Lösungen
* **Interkulturelle Kompetenz:** Vorbereitung auf die globalisierte Arbeitswelt und Zusammenarbeit in internationalen Teams

Unser Masterstudiengang zeichnet sich durch eine starke **Verknüpfung von Wissenschaft und Praxis** sowie **internationale Vernetzung** aus. Sie haben die Möglichkeit,

* ein eigenes Unternehmen mit digitalem Geschäftsmodell zu gründen,
* sich mit Ihrer Masterarbeit auf Ihre Interessengebiete zu konzentrieren
* und an **internationalen Programmen, Summer Schools oder Auslandsaufenthalten an Partnerhochschulen** teilzunehmen.

**Für wen ist der Studiengang geeignet?**

Der „Entrepreneurship and Corporate Venturing“-Master richtet sich an alle, die

* **unternehmerische Fähigkeiten** auf das nächste Level bringen möchten,
* Interesse an **Innovation, Digitalisierung** und **Geschäftsmodellentwicklung** haben,
* eine Karriere als **Gründer bzw. Gründerin** in der **Unternehmensberatung** oder im **Innovationsmanagement** anstreben.

**Machen Sie den nächsten Schritt in Ihrer Karriere!**

Werden Sie Teil unseres **internationalen Netzwerks** von Menschen mit **Visionen** und **Expertise**. Ob Sie ein eigenes Unternehmen gründen, ein Corporate Venture aufbauen oder Innovationen entwickeln – mit unserem Masterstudiengang „Entrepreneurship and Corporate Venturing“ legen Sie den Grundstein für Ihre erfolgreiche Zukunft.

## Studieninhalte

1.-3. Semester

Der Studiengang Entrepreneurship and Corporate Venturing umfasst zwei Vollzeit-Studiensemester vor Ort in Ingolstadt. Zulassung und Bewerbung

Für den Masterstudiengang Entrepreneurship and Corporate Venturing (ECV) müssen folgende Qualifikationen nachgewiesen werden:

* Der erfolgreiche Abschluss eines wirtschaftswissenschaftlichen oder artverwandten Studiums an einer deutschen Hochschule mit mindestens 210 ECTS-Leistungspunkten oder ein gleichwertiger in- oder ausländischer Abschluss. Der Nachweis wird durch Vorlage des Abschlusszeugnisses geführt. Über die Gleichwertigkeit entscheidet die Prüfungskommission.
* Ein erfolgreich absolviertes Eignungsverfahren ([Anlage 2 SPO](https://www.thi.de/hochschule/ueber-uns/hochschulorganisation/stabsstelle-recht/satzungen-business-school/masterstudiengaenge/spo-master-entrepreneurship-and-corporate-venturing/)).
* Aufgrund der internationalen Ausrichtung des Studiengangs ist der Nachweis folgender Sprachkenntnisse erforderlich: Englisch: B2 Level [(Übersicht: anerkannte Englischtests B2)](https://www.thi.de/hochschule/ueber-uns/hochschulorganisation/stabsstelle-recht/allgemeine-satzungen/#c26979)
* **Bitte beachten Sie: Erst nach Vorlage ihrer vollständigen Bewerbungsunterlagen wird Ihre Zulassung geprüft. Ohne Vorlage von Zeugnissen ist eine verbindliche Aussage nicht möglich. Vorabauskünfte per Mail oder Telefon über Chancen zur Zulassung können nicht gegeben werden.**

Hier finden Sie [Informationen zur Masterbewerbung](https://www.thi.de/studium/studienbewerbung/masterbewerbung/).

Der folgende Link enthält [Informationen für Bewerber/innen aus dem Ausland](https://www.thi.de/studium/studienbewerbung/masterbewerbungen-aus-dem-ausland/) zum Bewerbungsverfahren.

HINWEIS zu 180 ECTS: Kann ich mich bewerben, auch wenn ich mein bisheriges Bachelorstudium mit weniger als 210 ECTS (i.d.R. 180 ECTS) abgeschlossen habe?

Eine Bewerbung ist auch mit weniger als 210 ECTS möglich, jedoch müssen die Nachweise zum Ausgleich der fehlenden 30 ECTS bis spätestens zum Ende des Bewerbungszeitraums nachgewiesen werden durch:

* den Nachweis berufspraktischer Leistungen außerhalb des Studiums, welche inhaltlich und im Umfang dem Praxissemester eines wirtschaftswissenschaftlichen Bachelorstudiums entsprechen
**oder**
* den Nachweis zusätzlich zum Erstabschluss an einer in- oder ausländischen Hochschule erbrachter praktischer oder theoretischer Studien- und Prüfungsleistungen, welche inhaltlich und im Umfang den Anforderungen eines wirtschaftswissenschaftlichen Bachelorstudiums entsprechen.

Duales Studium oder Studium mit vertiefter Praxis

Das Duale Studium ermöglicht eine Kombination aus praktischen Ausbildungselementen in einem Unternehmen und theoretischer Ausbildung an der Hochschule. Dabei kann entweder das Verbundmodell (Studium & Berufsausbildung) oder der Studiengang mit vertiefter Praxis (Studium und intensive Praxisphasen) gewählt werden.

Der Einstieg im Unternehmen beginnt im Modell Verbundstudium unternehmensabhängig zum Teil bereits mehrere Monate vor dem ersten Semester. Mit Beginn des Studiums wechseln Theorie (Semesterzeiten) und Praxis (vorlesungsfreie Zeit sowie Praxissemester) einander ab. Für das duale Studium ist sowohl eine Bewerbung beim Unternehmen als auch an der Hochschule erforderlich (Achtung: Bewerbungsfristen im Unternehmen beachten!). Die THI unterstützt Studierende nicht bei der Suche nach Unternehmen für das duale Studium.

Bitte informieren Sie sich auf unseren Seiten zum [Dualen Studium](https://www.thi.de/studium/studienangebote/duales-studium/) bzw. auf der Suche nach einem Dualen Unternehmenspartner.

Berufsbilder

Unser Programm vermittelt den Studierenden die Denkweise, Fähigkeiten und Werkzeuge, um Chancen zu erkennen, innovative Geschäftsmodelle zu entwickeln und Unternehmen von der frühen Ideenfindung bis zum Wachstum zu führen. Die Studierenden werden auf eine Vielzahl von unternehmerischen Aufgaben vorbereitet:

* Gründer oder Mitbegründer eines Start-ups: Gründen und leiten Sie Ihr eigenes Unternehmen, von der Idee bis zur Markteinführung.
* Produktmanager in einem Start-up: Entwickeln Sie die Produktvision und Roadmap in einem dynamischen Start-up-Umfeld.
* Business Developer in einem Scaleup: Treiben Sie strategische Partnerschaften, Kundenakquise und Wachstumsinitiativen voran.
* Startup-Accelerator oder Incubator Associate: Unterstützen Sie Portfolio-Startups mit Mentoring, Programmgestaltung und Venture Building.

Wir bereiten die Studierenden auch darauf vor, als unternehmerische Changemaker innerhalb etablierter Organisationen zu agieren. Unsere Absolvierenden sind in der Lage, sich sowohl in Unternehmensstrukturen als auch in agilen Innovationsumgebungen zurechtzufinden:

* Innovationsmanagement: Leiten Sie Innovationsinitiativen, interne Unternehmen oder Innovationslabore innerhalb von Unternehmen.
* Unternehmensstrategie: Analysieren Sie Trends, suchen Sie nach Geschäftsmöglichkeiten und entwickeln Sie Innovationsstrategien.
* Intrapreneurship oder New Business Development: Entwickeln Sie neue Produkte, Dienstleistungen oder Geschäftsmodelle aus dem Unternehmen heraus.
* Venture Client oder Corporate VC – Verwalten Sie Partnerschaften und Investitionen in Start-ups, die mit den Unternehmenszielen übereinstimmen.
* Programm- oder Projektmanagement: Beaufsichtigen Sie funktionsübergreifende Innovationsprogramme und koordinieren Sie deren Umsetzung.

## FAQs

Was sind die Hauptthemen des Masterprogramms "Entrepreneurship and Corporate Venturing"?

Entrepreneurial Methods & Venture Projects, Lean Analytics, AI & Smart Data Science, Digital Business Technologies, Corporate Venturing & Innovation, Entrepreneurial Finance & Growth, Technology Commercialization & Intellectual Property Management, Global Entrepreneurship & Intercultural Leadership, Wahlpflichtfach

Was ist Corporate Venturing?

Corporate Venturing vermittelt Studierenden die Fähigkeit, unternehmerisches Denken in etablierten Organisationen anzuwenden. Ob durch interne Start-ups, Innovationslabore, Ausgründungen oder strategische Investitionen in Start-ups – Corporate Venturing treibt Erneuerung und Wachstum voran. Unser Programm schult Studierende darin, Chancen zu erkennen, neue Geschäftsmodelle zu entwickeln und Innovationsprozesse in Unternehmensumgebungen zu managen. Die Absolvierenden sind darauf vorbereitet, eigene Unternehmen zu gründen oder als Intrapreneure in Funktionen wie Strategie, Beratung, Projekt- oder Innovationsmanagement zu Innovationen beizutragen. Mit einer soliden Grundlage in Lean Analytics, digitalen Technologien und Wachstumsmanagement lernen die Studierenden, wie sie sowohl in Start-ups als auch in etablierten Unternehmen Wert schaffen können. Corporate Venturing schlägt eine Brücke zwischen Unternehmertum und der Unternehmenswelt und konzentriert sich auf die Entwicklung der nächsten Generation von Changemakern.

Muss ich ein Startup gründen, um diesen Studiengang abzuschließen?

Sie müssen am Ende dieses Studiums nicht unbedingt ein Start-Up gründen. Die pädagogische Herangehensweise des Programms wird Sie jedoch dazu anleiten, während Ihres Studiums die notwendigen Herausforderungen für die Gründung Ihres eigenen Unternehmens zu bewältigen.

Meine Bewerbung ist schon länger her, wann bekomme ich endlich meine Zusage?

In der Regel bemühen wir uns um eine zeitnahe Rückmeldung, es kann aber bis nach dem Bewerbungszeitraum dauern, bis Sie unsere finale Antwort erhalten. Bitte prüfen Sie Ihre Bewerbung im Primussportal regelmäßig, ob eventuell Dokumente nachgefordert wurden.

Kann ich während des Studiums arbeiten?

Es ist möglich, während des Semesters zu arbeiten. Die Pflichtvorlesungen finden überwiegend an drei festen Wochentagen statt, jedoch finden Blockveranstaltungen und Exkursionen an zusätzlichen Tagen statt. Ebenso können das Wahlpflichtfach an allen Tagen der Woche stattfinden. Räumen Sie sich daher genug Flexibilität bei Ihrem Arbeitgeber ein. Beachten Sie außerdem das hohe Lernpensum während des Studiums.

Mit welchem Vorstudium kann ich mich für ECV bewerben?

Zulassungsvoraussetzung ist der Nachweis eines erfolgreichen Abschlusses eines Studiums an einer deutschen Hochschule mit mindestens 210 ECTS-Leistungspunkten oder äquivalentem Studienumfang im Bereich Wirtschaftswissenschaften (oder artverwandt)  oder ein gleichwertiger erfolgreicher ausländischer Abschluss.

Was muss ich für das Eignungsverfahren in der Bewerbung vorbereiten?

Das Eignungsverfahren ist kein Test, für den gelernt werden muss. Bitte füllen Sie das Excel „Curricularanalyse“ wahrheitsgemäß und ggf. mit Erläuterungen aus und laden Sie es mit Ihren Bewerbungsunterlagen im Bewerberportal Primuss hoch. Die Informationen für das Eignungsverfahren werden aus der Curricularanalyse und Ihren weiteren Bewerbungsunterlagen entnommen. Die Kriterien entnehmen Sie bitte Anlage 2 der Studien- und Prüfungsordnung.

Kann ich einen Doppelabschluss machen?

Derzeit gibt es keine Kooperation für einen Doppelabschluss. Sie können allerdings einen Auslandsaufenthalt (z.B.: Semesterferien, Praktikum/Werkstudierendenaufenthalt, Masterarbeit) an einer der zahlreichen Auslandspartnerhochschulen durchführen und dort auch Fächer belegen.

Wann ist der Bewerbungszeitraum?

Bewerbungszeitraum für das Wintersemester: 2. Mai bis 15. Juli

In welcher Sprache finden die Vorlesungen statt?

Die Vorlesungen finden auf Englisch statt.

Mein Bachelorstudium war komplett auf Englisch, muss ich trotzdem noch ein Sprachzertifikat für Englisch machen?

Ein englisches Vorstudium reicht aus. Laden Sie hierzu im Bewerbungsportal bitte einen entsprechenden Nachweis hoch, der zeigt, dass die Vorlesungen auf Englisch waren. Hier finden Sie eine Übersicht der akzeptierten Sprachnachweise: [Link](https://www.thi.de/hochschule/ueber-uns/hochschulorganisation/stabsstelle-recht/allgemeine-satzungen/)

Welches Sprachniveau muss ich nachweisen können?

Sie müssen vor Beginn des Studiums Englischkenntnisse auf dem Niveau B2 gemäß dem Gemeinsamen Europäischen Referenzrahmen für Sprachen erworben haben. Nachweise hierfür sind zwingend zur Immatrikulation fällig, also innerhalb von vier Wochen nach Semesterstart. Hier finden Sie eine Übersicht der akzeptierten Sprachnachweise: [Link](https://www.thi.de/hochschule/ueber-uns/hochschulorganisation/stabsstelle-recht/allgemeine-satzungen/)

Brauche ich Deutschkenntnisse für das Studium?

Sie brauchen keine Deutschkenntnisse für dieses Studium. Falls Sie bisher keine Deutschkenntnisse haben, empfehlen wir natürlich, sich diese im Laufe des Studiums anzueignen. Hierzu stehen beispielsweise Deutschkurse unseres [Sprachenzentrums](https://www.thi.de/studium/sprachenzentrum/kursangebot-gesamt/) zur Verfügung.

Optional kann für das Wahlpflichtfach eine deutschsprachige Vorlesung besucht werden. Dies ist aber nur bei ausreichender Sprachkenntnis möglich. Die Vergabe der Wahlpflichtmodule erfolgt in Absprache mit der Studiengangleitung.

Wegen Visaproblemen kann ich erst nach Semesterstart in Ingolstadt sein, was hat das für Konsequenzen?

Es ist möglich verspätet anzureisen, allerdings muss die Immatrikulation trotzdem rechtzeitig erfolgen (spätestens vier Wochen nach Semesterstart). Verpassten Vorlesungsstoff müssen Sie eigenverantwortlich nachholen. Wir weisen jedoch ausdrücklich darauf hin, dass Ihre mit dem Studium einhergehenden Pflichten hiervon unberührt bleiben. Dies betrifft insbesondere die prüfungsrechtlichen Fristen, die hierdurch nicht unterbrochen werden. Wenn Sie erst mehrere Wochen nach Semesterbeginn ankommen, wird es nicht möglich sein, alle Module des ersten Semesters nachzuholen.

## 1 Overview

## 2 Introduction

Entrepreneurship is more than starting a company, it is about turning ideas into impact. This Master of Science in Entrepreneurship and Corporate Venturing program prepares you to do exactly that. It equips you with advanced academic knowledge, a practical mindset, and the international outlook necessary to thrive in today's rapidly changing business world.

At THI Business School, we believe that entrepreneurship must be practiced, not just studied. That is why you will work extensively on either your own startup project or on a corporate innovation challenge. Combined with academic rigor, close industry ties, and a strong technology orientation, this program offers a learning journey that is as demanding as it is rewarding.

### 2.1 Objectives

This program aims to prepare graduates for entrepreneurial careers in both startups and established organizations. To succeed in these environments, students need more than just enthusiasm. Students require methodical tools, strategic insights, and the ability to navigate uncertainty. Our objectives with the program are therefore twofold.

(1) We want to provide a solid foundation in theories, strategies, and methods of entrepreneurship and corporate innovation, enabling students to analyze and solve complex problems from multiple perspectives.

(2) We aim to foster entrepreneurial action through experiential learning, ensuring that students test, refine, and apply their ideas in real-world contexts.

Through this combination, graduates are equipped not only to create ventures of their own but also to become valuable innovators and intrapreneurs in established companies.

### 2.2 Admission Requirements

Applicants are expected to hold a bachelor's degree in a business-related subject (such as business administration, management, entrepreneurship, or innovation management) or a comparable qualification from a recognized institution. A minimum of 210 ECTS is usually required.

Applicants with a bachelor's degree of 180 ECTS credits may also be admitted. In this case, however, they must provide proof of how the missing 30 ECTS credits are compensated. This proof must be submitted no later than the end of the application period. Compensation for the missing credits can be demonstrated in one of the following ways:

- Practical experience: Documented professional experience completed outside the degree program, equivalent in content and scope to the practical semester of a bachelor’s degree in business.
- Additional academic work: Proof of practical or theoretical coursework and examinations successfully completed at a recognized German or foreign university, equivalent in content and scope to modules of a bachelor’s degree in business.

All applicants complete an aptitude assessment, which considers their academic performance, entrepreneurial experience, and relevant coursework. This is not a written test but a structured evaluation of the applicant's fit for the program. The evaluation is based on a form that applicants complete during the application process.

As the program is taught entirely in English, a proficiency of at least level B2 of the Common European Framework of Reference is required. While not mandatory, learning German is strongly recommended to facilitate integration into the regional ecosystem.

The general admission requirements for THI master's programs can also be found at: https://www.thi.de/en/studies/application/master-application.

### 2.3 Target Group

The M.Sc. in Entrepreneurship and Corporate Venturing is designed for students who see their future in the world of innovation, entrepreneurship, and business transformation. It appeals to those who are eager to go beyond conventional management studies and want to learn how to create, grow, and sustain new ventures. Many of our students join with the ambition of founding their own startup, while others are particularly motivated by the idea of driving change inside established organizations. What unites them is a strong curiosity for new ideas, the willingness to work in teams, and the desire to actively shape the future of business.

The program is particularly well-suited for graduates of business-related bachelor's degrees who now wish to build a more profound expertise in entrepreneurship and innovation. At the same time, it also attracts students from more specialized areas, such as innovation management, digital business, or international management, who want to broaden their horizons and complement their previous studies with a stronger entrepreneurial orientation.

Students who choose this master’s program often have different professional goals in mind. Some aspire to launch their own startup and see this degree as a unique opportunity to refine their business ideas, test them in practice, and build a network of like-minded peers and mentors. Others envision themselves joining a young and growing company in roles such as product management or business development, where they can contribute to scaling the venture. Another group is drawn to intrapreneurship, aspiring to become change agents within established firms, working on corporate innovation projects, new business models, or digital transformation initiatives. The program also prepares graduates for roles in consulting, venture capital, or innovation ecosystems, where entrepreneurial thinking is increasingly valued.

What connects these career paths is the ability to combine theoretical knowledge with practical skills, to act confidently in uncertain environments, and to collaborate across disciplines and cultures. The
program, therefore, welcomes students who are ambitious, open-minded, and motivated to use entrepreneurship as a tool for making a meaningful impact, whether in their own company, in a dynamic startup, or within a global corporation.

### 2.4 Program Structure

The program’s systematic structure enables students to gradually develop competencies, test them in real-world entrepreneurial projects, and ultimately apply them in their own ventures or corporate innovation settings. The curriculum is organized into five interconnected clusters, each contributing to the program’s distinctive profile.

The first cluster, Entrepreneurial Competences, provides the theoretical and methodological foundations of entrepreneurship. Students are introduced to contemporary Entrepreneurial Methods, learn to critically evaluate theories and models, and develop their own stance towards entrepreneurship. They also acquire the financial and strategic skills necessary for building and scaling new ventures through the module on Entrepreneurial Finance and Growth Management.

The second cluster, Corporate Venturing and Digital Business, focuses on how established organizations innovate. Students explore corporate venturing strategies, learn to navigate internal and external innovation ecosystems, and analyze the role of digital technologies in shaping new business models. The international dimension of entrepreneurship is addressed through the module on Global Entrepreneurship and Intercultural Leadership, which prepares students to operate confidently in diverse and cross-cultural contexts.

The third cluster, Business Analytics, equips students with the ability to make data-driven decisions as an essential skill in today's entrepreneurial world. Modules such as Lean Analytics and AI & Smart Data Science demonstrate how to leverage data for venture development and growth. An Elective course further allows students to tailor their studies to their individual interests, choosing from a range of modules at THI or even at international partner universities.

The fourth cluster, Entrepreneurship & Venture Project, spans the first two semesters and serves as the program’s experiential learning backbone. Here, students work in teams either on their own startup idea. Each stage provides a structured way to apply theoretical learning to practice, supported by coaching, mentoring, and feedback from experts.

Finally, the fifth cluster includes Research Methods for Business and the Master's Thesis. In the research methods module, students strengthen their academic and analytical skills, which form the basis for writing their thesis. The thesis itself, accompanied by a final colloquium, is the culminating project of the program. It enables students to demonstrate their ability to apply academic knowledge and methodological rigor to a complex entrepreneurial challenge, often by building upon their venture project.

### 2.6 Program Principles

The guiding idea of the program is that entrepreneurship is best learned through hands-on experience, supported by a strong academic foundation. Its curriculum design rests on several principles.

- Scientific foundation: Students engage with current research, theories, and frameworks to develop strong analytical and methodological skills.

- Experiential learning: Through the venture project, students practice entrepreneurship, learn to experiment, reflect on outcomes, and adapt their strategies. Guest speakers, mentoring, and coaching support this hands-on experience.

- Technology focus: Given THI’s strong technological orientation, the program integrates modules on digital technologies and data science, enabling students to critically assess and apply new technologies in business.

- Ecosystem engagement: Students are embedded in THI’s entrepreneurial ecosystem, including the Startup Center, student clubs, and partners such as brigk, a hub for digital startups located on campus.

- Global perspective: International guest lecturers, diverse student cohorts, and optional study-abroad opportunities foster intercultural competence and a global outlook.

In short, the program combines academic knowledge, practical experience, and access to the ecosystem to shape entrepreneurial leaders who can thrive in both startups and corporations.

#### 3.2.2 Program-Specific Competencies

Second, the program emphasizes venture growth and entrepreneurial finance. Students learn how to secure funding, design growth strategies, and manage the financial complexities of scaling startups. Third, graduates develop competencies in corporate venturing and innovation. This gives them the ability to leverage technology in building scalable and future-oriented business models.

Finally, the program is designed to foster an international and intercultural perspective on entrepreneurship. Through global case studies, international guest lecturers, and diverse student teams, graduates build the capacity to navigate cultural differences and seize opportunities in global markets.

#### 3.2.3 Entrepreneurship & Venturing Capstone Projects

A defining feature of the program are the Entrepreneurship & Venture Projects, a series of three interconnected modules that span the first two semesters. These courses serve as capstone experiences, bringing together insights from all other modules and providing students with a platform to practice entrepreneurship in real time.

In the first stage, students engage in problem discovery and opportunity spotting, whether it is for their own startup concept or a corporate innovation challenge. They apply methods of opportunity recognition, design thinking, and early validation. The focus lies on shaping possible project roadmaps and defining initial assumptions.

The second stage focuses on validation, refinement, and early market traction. Students conduct experiments, test prototypes, and evaluate feedback from stakeholders. Here, the knowledge gained in finance, digital business, and data analytics is directly applied to assess the viability of each idea.

The third stage moves toward implementation and scaling. Students refine their business model, develop a growth strategy, and prepare for potential market entry or corporate adoption. This stage culminates in a comprehensive presentation of results, often in front of experts, mentors, and ecosystem partners.

These project courses embody the program’s commitment to experiential learning. They require students to experiment, reflect, and adapt, mirroring the real-world process of entrepreneurship. By continuously integrating insights from other modules into their project work, students not only strengthen
their academic learning but also develop the resilience and creativity needed to thrive in entrepreneurial careers.

#### 3.2.4 Interdisciplinary Competencies

One key interdisciplinary competence is the ability to navigate complexity and uncertainty. Students learn heuristics for decision-making under incomplete information, develop tolerance for ambiguity, and acquire the resilience to pursue long-term goals despite short-term setbacks. These skills are critical in entrepreneurial ventures but equally valuable in corporate and consulting environments.

Another is collaborative problem-solving. Students work in diverse, multicultural teams where they must align perspectives, manage conflicts, and leverage each member's strengths. This fosters leadership skills, empathy, and the ability to communicate across disciplines and cultures.

The program also builds analytical and data literacy competencies that cut across domains. From lean analytics to AI-driven decision-making, students acquire the ability to interpret data, identify patterns, and draw actionable conclusions, which are skills that are highly relevant to startups, corporations, and policy organizations alike.

Furthermore, graduates develop a mindset of creativity and opportunity recognition. They are encouraged to challenge assumptions, explore alternative solutions, and remain open to innovation wherever it emerges. This creative competence is not confined to entrepreneurship but enhances performance in strategic management, product development, and organizational leadership.

Finally, the program strengthens ethical awareness and social responsibility. Students reflect on the societal implications of entrepreneurial decisions, from sustainability to digital ethics. This equips them to contribute responsibly to businesses and communities in their future careers.

### 3.4 Possible Career Paths

Graduates of the Master’s in Entrepreneurship and Corporate Venturing are prepared for a broad range of professional fields. The program equips them with entrepreneurial competence, analytical capabilities, and leadership skills that can be applied across startups, established companies, and the wider innovation ecosystem. With its strong combination of academic rigor and experiential learning, the program opens up diverse career opportunities both in Germany and internationally.

Many graduates choose to pursue their own entrepreneurial ventures. They benefit from having developed their ideas throughout the Entrepreneurship & Venture Project, gaining first-hand experience of the challenges and rewards of building a business. Others prefer to join innovative companies, where they can take on strategic roles in developing products, scaling operations, or driving digital transformation. For students interested in combining entrepreneurial spirit with corporate resources, career paths in corporate venturing and intrapreneurship provide particularly attractive opportunities.

The following fields represent the most common and promising career paths for graduates:

- Startup Founder or Co-Founder: Launching a new venture based on innovative business models or technologies, either as a continuation of the student’s own project or by joining a founding team.

- Startup Team Member in Early-Stage Ventures: Contributing expertise in areas such as business development, operations, or finance in a fast-paced, high-growth environment.

- Corporate Venturing Specialist or Innovation Manager: Driving innovation projects within established companies, leading intrapreneurial initiatives, and building bridges between startups and corporations.

- Digital Transformation and Strategy Roles: Helping companies adapt to technological change by developing new digital business models and implementing data-driven strategies.

- Consulting and Advisory: Working with management consultancies, innovation agencies, or accelerators to support entrepreneurs, corporate innovators, and investors with expertise in entrepreneurship and venture growth.

- Venture Capital and Investment Management: Supporting venture capital firms, corporate investors, or startup incubators in identifying, evaluating, and mentoring promising new ventures.

- Research and Academic Careers: Applying advanced research methods in entrepreneurship and innovation, potentially leading to doctoral studies or research positions in academia or applied research institutions.

By offering a wide spectrum of opportunities, the program ensures that graduates can tailor their career paths to their individual ambitions, whether that means building their own company, driving innovation within an established firm, or contributing to the entrepreneurial ecosystem as consultants, investors, or researchers.
## 4 Dual Studies

The Master’s in Entrepreneurship and Corporate Venturing is primarily designed as a full-time study program. Most students choose this standard format, as it allows them to fully immerse themselves in their studies, focus on their venture project, and make the most of the program’s international and entrepreneurial ecosystem.

In addition to the full-time option, students may also choose to pursue the degree as a dual study program. This pathway combines academic coursework with extended professional experience in a company. To take part, students must secure a participating partner company themselves. The university does not assign or provide company partners. Instead, it is the responsibility of each student to approach suitable companies and formalize a dual study agreement.

A number of companies are already approved as dual studies partner companies at THI. In addition, new companies can apply for approval if they wish to collaborate with the program and host dual students in the future. To ensure feasibility, the location of the partner company's office should be close enough to Ingolstadt to allow students to commute between campus and company site.

The dual study format offers the advantage of working intensively within a company while completing the master’s program. This often includes writing the master’s thesis in collaboration with the partner company, thereby directly linking academic research with practical business challenges. However, because of the additional commitments, the dual pathway typically requires more organization and effort than the standard study mode. Dual students spend at least 34 weeks or 8.5 months (including the master’s thesis) at their respective company.

Further information on the dual studies option for master’s programs at THI can be accessed here: https://www.thi.de/en/studies/degree-programmes/dual-studies/master-dual/

More information for companies about how to become a dual studies partner can be found here: https://www.thi.de/en/service/dual-studies-information-for-companies/

In accordance with THI’s overarching concept for the design of dual study programs, the integration of university and company learning environments is based on three key aspects: legal/contractual integration, organizational integration, and content-related integration.

## Legal Integration

The special features of the dual study program are defined in §29(3) and §30(5) of the APO as well as §8b of the THI Enrollment Statutes. Accordingly:

- When enrolling at THI, the training contract (for the combined study program) or the education contract (for in-depth practical training) with the partner company must be submitted by the end of the first semester at the latest.
The final thesis must be written in cooperation with the partner company under the academic supervision of the university. At least one formal exchange between the university supervisor and the company is required. The main results of the thesis must be presented both to the partner company and to the supervising professor by the dual student.

## Organizational Integration

Organizational integration is ensured through regular contact between dual students, THI, and the partner companies. The following contact points are institutionalized:

An information session in the first semester for all dual students, led by the Dean of Studies.

- An annual meeting of all dual students in the degree program, organized by the program director.

- A yearly symposium for dual partner companies, organized by the THI Career Service (“Forum Dual”).

- The program director serves as the central contact for the entire dual study program and also acts as a mentor for dual students.

## Content-Related Integration

Content-related integration ensures that the knowledge and skills taught in the program are directly applicable within the company, and that practical experience feeds back into the academic studies. In the M.Sc. Entrepreneurship and Corporate Venturing, this integration takes place primarily through the master's thesis, which counts for 25 ECTS credits. According to §30(5), the thesis must be completed within the partner company, guaranteeing academic exchange between the university and the company. The results of the thesis must be presented both to the university supervisor and to the company partner.

## Content:

### 1.1 : Entrepreneurial Methods:

This course provides a structured overview of key themes, theories, and strategies in contemporary entrepreneurship. It enables students to critically reflect on entrepreneurial action, decision-making, and venture development across different contexts. The course is organized into four thematic parts, each spanning two to three weeks. Theoretical inputs serve as a foundation for in-class discussions, applied exercises, and selected take-home assignments.

## Part 1: Entrepreneurship Today

This part introduces entrepreneurship as a societal, economic, and organizational phenomenon. It explores different forms of entrepreneurship, contrasts startups with established organizations, and examines entrepreneurial ecosystems with a particular focus on Germany and international contexts.

## Part 2: Being an Entrepreneur

This part focuses on the individual and team level of entrepreneurship. It examines entrepreneurial mindsets, traits, behaviors, and beliefs, as well as team dynamics and collaboration in entrepreneurial settings. A dedicated emphasis is placed on dealing with uncertainty and ambiguity as a core entrepreneurial capability.

## Part 3: Theories in Entrepreneurship

This part provides an overview of influential entrepreneurship theories and frameworks. It traces the historical development of entrepreneurship theory and introduces contemporary approaches such as Lean Startup, Effectuation, Design Thinking, and Disciplined Entrepreneurship, highlighting their assumptions, strengths, and limitations.

## Part 4: Basic Concepts of Startup Development

This part addresses foundational concepts of venture creation and early growth. It covers typical startup development stages, data-driven and digital business models, funding sources and investor roles, basic growth considerations, and approaches to assessing the potential future value of entrepreneurial ventures.

## Content:

### 3.1 : Lean Analytics:

This course introduces students to Lean Startup as a foundation for systematic learning under uncertainty and then develops a comprehensive Lean Analytics skill set for early-stage ventures and corporate innovation contexts. Students learn how to translate assumptions into testable hypotheses, design meaningful metrics, run structured experiments, and interpret data to support entrepreneurial decision-making. A particular emphasis is placed on experimentation, learning velocity, and evidence-based pivot or persevere decisions. The course further expands the analytical toolkit through pretotyping and prototyping approaches that enable fast, ethical, and cost-efficient learning.

## Theme 1: Lean Startup Foundations

• Core principles of Lean Startup and hypothesis-driven entrepreneurship

• Differences between entrepreneurial experimentation and traditional planning-based management

• Learning under uncertainty and the logic of the build–measure–learn feedback loop

• Aligning product development and experimentation with learning objectives

## Theme 2: Lean Analytics

• Translating assumptions into testable hypotheses and learning goals

• Designing actionable learning metrics and avoiding vanity metrics

• Establishing baselines and benchmarks for early-stage decision-making

• Designing and interpreting structured experiments, including A/B tests

- Using analytical insights to support pivot, persevere, or kill decisions

## Theme 3: Pretotyping and Prototyping for Learning

• Purpose and limits of prototyping and prototyping in early-stage ventures

- Overview of common prototyping and prototyping strategies and tools

• Ethical considerations in experimentation with users and stakeholders

• Integrating Lean Analytics into prototyping and experimentation processes

## Content:

### 3.3 : Digital Analytics and Artificial Intelligence in Retailing

- Approximately 1/3 of each lesson will be devoted to the underlying analytics theory, to selected use cases within the field of retailing and to hands-on problem solving with open source software.

- We cover methods of data exploration, inference and hypothesis testing, clustering and segmentation, visualization and storytelling, regression, decision trees, experiments, testing and causation, recommendation engines, big data, machine learning and AI.

- Google Analytics will be introduced as the current market dominating digital analytics tool. Utilizing Google Analytics, students will learn how to use Digital Analytic software technology in the context of analytical, exploratory and reporting capabilities. Students will learn by doing: that is, guided by the instructor and using software, they will focus on data discovery and communicating insights.

- Open source code will be provided as Google Collaboratory notebooks for best traceability and learning support for IT-savvy as well as non-IT-savvy students.

### 3.3 : Strategic Brand Development & Go-to-Market Management:

The module follows a structured brand-building process consisting of five interconnected stages (5D Method):

1. Discover: Re-assesss prior work, analyzing the existing startup idea's market and customer insight using secondary and primary research data; identification of core customer jobs and pain points for further usage in the brand development. Students without an existing business model idea will use this timeframe for initial set-up.

2. Define: Segmentation, targeting, and positioning in the context of new ventures; developing a clear brand route and audience understanding.

3. Develop: Creation of the brand identity (e.g., using Kapferer's Brand Identity Prism) and definition of major building blocks such as naming, brand character, and tone of voice.

## Content:

3.3: SUS - Sustainable HR Management:

The course will examine different aspects about sustainable HR Management and health management.

• Students analyse the role of corporate sustainability and CSR for the human resource management practice.

• Students reflect and evaluate the different practices in health management.

• Students develop and produce a research paper that explains a particular issue or phenomenon of the discussion on sustainable HR and health management.

Based on a 360 degrees approach to skills development, students will be actively involved in the development and evaluation of presentation skills. They will provide feedback on their colleague's presentations after every session and will reflect and evaluate the outcome of their presentation using a self-assessment instrument.
