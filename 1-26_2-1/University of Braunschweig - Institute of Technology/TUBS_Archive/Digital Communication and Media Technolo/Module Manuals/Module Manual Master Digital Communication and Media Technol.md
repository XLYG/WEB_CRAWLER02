## Contents

Subject-specific content from the two core areas of digital communication and media technologies is presented in a lecture series, in particular on the basis of current research projects. In the group and under the guidance of a tutorial, students draw connections between the two teaching and research areas. The aim is to emphasise the connection between the two areas at the beginning of the degree course and to promote interface skills.

## Objective qualification

The aim of the interdisciplinary module is to clarify the connection between the core areas of digital communication and media technologies right at the beginning of the degree programme, to convey the added value of interdisciplinary thinking and to promote interface skills. At the same time, students are given an initial understanding of the different disciplinary perspectives on the subjects of the degree programme. The interdisciplinary module thus also forms a basis for the students' own specialisation in the further course of their studies.

## Contents

In the course "Digital Communication Research" (lecture or seminar), the various social dimensions of digital communication are presented. Forms of digital communication affect practices of everyday information behaviour, cultural participation and relationship management as well as the work of professional communicators in journalism, politics or PR, both within their respective organisations and in relation to their respective communicative reference groups. They change the structures of the social public sphere and break up the dominance of the "mass communication" mode and its institutionalised forms by providing other modes, arenas and levels that make communication content visible and accessible to a (potentially) large group of people. This in turn also has an impact on the understanding of privacy and informational self-determination, because it becomes more difficult to control and delimit audiences for personal information. The lecture provides an overview of the current state of research and discussion.

## Objective qualification

After completing the module, students will have an overview of the "state of the art" of the utilisation practices, social embedding and effects of digital communication. In addition to social media, this also includes new forms of human-computer interaction and human-machine communication. Students acquire indepth knowledge of digital communication in selected areas of society and are able to describe and reflect on these from a communication science perspective. They will be able to critically categorise and evaluate the opportunities and risks of various forms of digital communication for individuals and society. Students will thus have the skills required for well-founded theoretical reflection and analysis of digital forms of communication, which are necessary in all professional fields of communication (e.g. journalism, public relations, sales, advertising and marketing) now and in the future.

## Contents

The course "Fundamentals of Science Communication" (lecture or seminar) provides an overview of the current state of theory and research on the various forms of science communication: journalistic communication about science, interpersonal communication between laypeople about science and the communication of science. The latter can take place externally, i.e. publicly and across fields, or internally, formally or informally.

Current research findings are linked with communication science theories. The focus is on theories of content and journalism research, usage, reception and impact research as well as the increasing medialisation of science. This provides a comprehensive insight into the field of research and focuses on the changing understanding of science and the public.

In the Science Communication seminar, the relationship between science and the public is examined in depth, focussing in particular on media reporting and lay communication on scientific topics. Current scientific topics alternate as the subject of theoretical and empirical analyses. The aim of the seminar is to analyse both the presentation of a specific scientific topic in the mass media and social communication on and offline on this topic. In addition to the current state of research, the theories of communication science content and journalism research as well as utilisation, reception and impact research will be used and applied to a specific science communication context.

## Objective qualification

After completing the module, students will be familiar with the current state of theory and research on science communication. Various forms of science communication are considered, i.e. any external (public), internal or interpersonal communication on scientific topics. Students are thus able to analyse science communication in a theoretically sound manner, they are familiar with current findings from national and international research and can critically categorise communication on scientific topics.

These skills are relevant for their own research work and also enable them to carry out tasks focussing on the analytical examination of various forms of science communication.

## Objective qualification

The goal is that students can derive the main information theoretic results on maximal achievable lossless (source coding) and lossy (rate distortion theory) compression of data and on maximum data rates for reliable data transmission (channel coding). The methods and tools required, e.g., information measures (entropy, mutual information, capacity etc.) and their properties (typical sequences) will be covered as well as practical applicable simple codes (block, turbo and polar codes).

## Contents

The subject of the module is media analysis and media interpretation using examples from as broadly differentiated areas of media production as possible. The material for formal, content-related and discursive analyses and interpretations should come from different media, genres, historical periods and cultural circles. A broad spectrum of methodological diversity will be used, and the results of analytical investigations will be subjected to reflection and interpretation and placed in broader (inter-)disciplinary contexts. The lecturers' current objects of research and methods of analysis are systematically incorporated into teaching and module examinations, e.g. in the past from the DFG project 'The Poison Motif in Film' in the course of the same name.

## Objective qualification

The aim is to achieve a differentiated understanding and increased aesthetic sensitisation to a wide variety of media forms. In addition, the methods and analytical approaches learnt in the BA are deepened and practised for the purpose of transferring their results into plausibly argued interpretation and clear linguistic presentation. The interest in knowledge is focussed on levels of depth and contextual meanings of media products. Discursive forms of learning such as presentations, group work, seminar discussions, predominantly based on media reception. The exercise serves to deepen individual key topics.

- know, remember and understand current concepts, procedures and methods for analysing media products and contexts and can apply and evaluate them to media objects and phenomena.
- know, remember and understand current research projects and are able to relate them to what they have learnt and actively participate in them if necessary.
- can clearly present, communicate, acquire and critically evaluate knowledge discursively by means of presentation, discussion and argumentation, including cooperative forms of learning and group work.

Professional Competences: Students are able to understand, classify, evaluate and critically reflect on media studies analyses and current research questions and designs.

Interdisciplinary Competences: Students are able to understand and evaluate analytical perspectives and to analyse complex content, objects and constellations alone and in collaborative groups and to present, discuss, appropriate and critically evaluate the results.

## Contents

Selected theoretical complexes and discourses are used to reflect on specific problems in media theory. Possible topics include cultural studies/history and theory of mass media, digitality as a 'meta-medium' in media history, media and subjective experience, changes in the concept of the public sphere, transparency vs. manipulation in different phases of media history, comparative approaches to reception theory. The lecturers' current research topics, for example from the fields of game studies, film studies or postcolonial studies, are systematically incorporated into teaching and module examinations, e.g. in the past on the DFG project 'Kulturtechnik Unternehmensplanspiel' in the advanced seminar 'Kulturtechnik Spiel'. The mediatheoretical core can be expanded in two directions: on the one hand by adding basic and background texts that are relevant to the respective media-theoretical approaches, and on the other hand by transferring them to meta-levels with a philosophical orientation. Media-historical approaches are analysed and interpreted with regard to their theoretical implications. Theories are given a historical perspective, history is interpreted as relevant to theory.

## Objective qualification

The handling of theoretical and historical texts should now go beyond their purely content-related level and be deepened, above all with regard to the development of competences for the independent and critical discussion of media science issues and research designs. Students should no longer be able to deal with media studies problems within a narrow framework, but to place them in broader contexts and analyse them at a deeper level.

## The students

- know, remember and understand current media theoretical and historical topics, approaches and concepts and are able to analyse, relate and evaluate them.
- know, remember and understand current research projects and are able to relate them to what they have learnt and, if necessary, actively participate in them.
- can communicate, acquire and critically evaluate knowledge discursively by means of presentation, discussion and argumentation, including cooperative forms of learning and group work.

Professional Competences: Students are able to understand, categorise, evaluate and critically reflect on media studies theory formation and historiography as well as current research questions and designs.

Interdisciplinary Competences: Students are able to understand complex theories and historical issues and to present, discuss, appropriate and critically evaluate complex content alone and in collaborative groups.

## Contents

- Models and methods of political communication and policy analysis
- Case studies of political communication and policy field analysis, especially in the areas of health, welfare state, employment and education

## Objective qualification

The political science module "Politics and Media" teaches skills that are in demand in fields such as political communication, for example in public relations, political consulting and public affairs. The module is also aimed at students who wish to acquire different perspectives for independent academic research. The academic foundation is laid by teaching selected theoretical and methodological approaches to political communication and policy field analysis. Students acquire the ability to apply these concepts to different contexts, particularly in the fields of media, health, education, labour and employment. The diverse interdisciplinary methodological approaches of policy field analysis are also analysed with reference to media and communication science concepts. Basically, the module imparts skills in understanding, presenting and categorising scientific results.

## Contents

The module aims to reflect on the development and technical design of media and media systems primarily from a media cultural studies, sociological, historical, philosophical and political perspective. Media development should not be understood here primarily as a linear process of innovation or the discovery of specific technologies. It is not distinct technical objects that represent the actual meaning-creating and meaning-producing core of the project of informatic, media or visual culture, but rather social structures of action on technologies. At the centre of the module are diverse opportunities to understand media technologies as largescale technical systems and social practices and to examine their genesis and impact using tools such as technology theory, sociology of technology, historiography and discourse-theoretical analysis.

## Objective qualification

The module trains students to relate technologies and media effects. Historiographical development studies provide a better understanding of innovation discourses and the social significance of media. Explicitly interdisciplinary approaches expand the ability to recognise media objects and phenomena as contextually embedded. The ability to critically scrutinise the (alleged) factual should be deepened.

## The students

- know, remember and understand current concepts, procedures and methods of technology theory and can combine them with media theoretical, historical and analytical approaches and apply them to innovation discourses, media objects and phenomena.
- can impart, acquire and critically evaluate knowledge discursively by means of presentation, discussion and argumentation, including cooperative forms of learning and group work.

Specialist Competences: Students are able to theorise, historicise and analyse interdisciplinary media-technical and -cultural phenomena as well as to understand, classify, evaluate and critically reflect on current research questions and designs.

Interdisciplinary Competences: Students are able to think and work in an interdisciplinary manner and to present, discuss, appropriate and critically evaluate complex theories and analyses, both alone and in collaborative groups.

## Contents

In the exercise on science communication practice, various forms of internal and/or external science communication are presented and designed. The exercise includes the development, realisation and presentation of a concept for the publication of scientific findings (e.g. in the form of a radio report) and/or for communication within science. The exercise is carried out by lecturers with established knowledge of the practical field of science communication. A total of two different exercises must be completed.

## Objective qualification

Students learn about the basic formats of applied science communication and create their own communication products in the form of texts, images or videos. They learn how different communication formats are created and acquire the relevant technical skills (e.g. handling a camera and microphone). This gives students a very good insight into the practical field of science communication and enables them to make their first contacts in the professional world through lecturers.
